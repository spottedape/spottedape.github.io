# Editorial vocabulary

Lead with what clients decide, produce or learn. The Spotted Ape’s contribution is its application and integration of behavioural design, strategic thinking, practical learning and human judgement.

## Services and outputs

| Service | Preferred output language |
| --- | --- |
| Strategic Decision Workshop | One-page record of strategic choices and assumptions |
| Knowledge Sharing & Organisational Learning Lab | Knowledge-flow and organisational-learning blueprint |
| Strategy Prototyping Studio | Alternative hypotheses, scenario prototypes and a plan for testing priority assumptions |
| Executive Offsites & Custom Masterclasses | Outputs agreed around the decision and participants |
| Strategic Decisions & Organisational Learning Masterclass | Strategic choices, assumptions to test and capabilities to develop |

Use “working output” in short descriptions. Distinguish a document completed during a workshop from a strategy validated by evidence or an organisational outcome achieved afterwards.

## Intellectual provenance

- Name the **Strategy Choice Cascade** when explaining or teaching the framework. On its first substantive use on a page, acknowledge *Playing to Win* by A.G. Lafley and Roger Martin: https://rogerlmartin.com/lets-read/playing-to-win.
- When deliberately using “What would have to be true?” as a strategic inquiry technique, acknowledge Roger Martin and explain that participants identify conditions an option requires and evidence that could challenge them: https://rogerlmartin.substack.com/p/the-evolution-of-the-strategic-choice-structuring-process-653521519014.
- Accessible paraphrases of the five cascade questions remain adaptations of the established framework. Renaming a service does not remove the need for attribution.
- Do not describe an engagement as the complete Strategic Choice Structuring Process unless its actual curriculum supports that description.
- Retain relevant acknowledgements of John Seely Brown and other intellectual influences. Do not invent bibliographic details, certification, endorsement or partnership.
- Ordinary language such as strategy, choice, decision, capability and trade-off does not require attribution by itself.
- Do not infer use of Integrative Thinking from combining disciplines or from the !Khairos role “Integrator”.

## Preferred wording

| Concept | Public-facing wording |
| --- | --- |
| Winning aspiration | What success means for the organisation |
| Where to play | Where to compete; which customers, needs, markets or channels to focus on |
| How to win | Why customers will choose the organisation over alternatives |
| Capabilities | What the organisation must be able to do well |
| Management systems | Management practices, measures and decision processes |
| Assumption inquiry | Conditions each option requires; assumptions to test |
| Judging Hat | Stakeholder review |
| !Khairos | Capability progression; working framework |

Preserve competitive exclusions, the relationships between choices and the supporting management arrangements. Accessible language should retain the precision of the concept.

## Voice and claims

Use British/South African spelling: judgement, organisation, behavioural, personalised and artefact. Preserve published titles, quotations, code identifiers and URL destinations.

Prefer concrete verbs: decide, compare, clarify, record, test, assign responsibility and review. Reserve “proprietary” for substantiated claims about specific material or technology. Describe our facilitation, application and workshop design without claiming ownership of established ideas.

State who can inspect, challenge, stop or approve AI-supported work and who remains accountable. Do not equate a human review exercise with empirical validation. Publish numerical improvements, guarantees or measured effects only with supporting evidence and scope.

Use consistent calls to action such as “Discuss a strategic challenge”, “Discuss this workshop” and “View workshop details”. Editorial label changes should preserve existing destinations and behaviour.
