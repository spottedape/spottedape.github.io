# The Spotted Ape: Content Strategy

## Positioning

The Spotted Ape helps leadership teams make consequential strategic choices under uncertainty and develop the organisational capabilities to act on them. The practice combines strategy as choice, learning through work and human judgment about evidence, consequences and responsibility.

AI is an important change to the conditions of work and competition, not a compulsory theme for every engagement. Where it matters, the work asks what can be delegated, what must remain a human judgment, what expertise people still need to practise and who is accountable for an outcome.

The existing hero question, “If AI does the work, what are you doing?”, remains a distinctive invitation into the issue of judgment and capability. Its explanation now connects that issue to executive choices, organisational learning and practical workshops.

## Audiences

- Executive leadership teams and general managers making or revisiting strategic choices.
- Strategy directors and business-unit leaders responsible for where to play, how to win and the capabilities needed.
- Innovation and product leaders testing alternatives beyond established assumptions.
- Organisational transformation, operations and learning leaders working with knowledge flows and adaptive capability.
- Organisations confronting AI-driven changes to work, competition, professional development or accountability.

Participants should have a relationship to the decision, work system or learning context being examined. Audience profile and influence shape each engagement; the site does not assume every workshop is suitable for every buyer.

## Value proposition

The practice does more than facilitate planning conversations or introduce frameworks. It helps a team:

- make linked strategic choices and trade-offs explicit;
- state what would have to be true for a strategic hypothesis to work;
- identify evidence, scenarios or bounded experiments that could inform a decision;
- connect codified knowledge to tacit expertise, practice and feedback;
- develop learning conditions such as shared work, communities of practice and creation spaces;
- leave with a working artifact that records choices, assumptions, learning design or next questions.

Artifacts are workshop outputs to refine and use, not finished proprietary products or guarantees of implementation, alignment or competitive advantage.

## Intellectual foundations and how they connect

### Strategy as choice

Roger Martin's choice cascade informs the sequence from winning aspiration to where to play, how to win, required capabilities and enabling management systems. Design thinking and abductive reasoning help teams formulate alternatives and hypotheses under uncertainty. “What would have to be true?” turns assumptions into questions that may affect a choice. The framework remains attributed to its source; the site describes how The Spotted Ape applies the ideas, not a proprietary invention.

### Scalable organisational learning

John Seely Brown's work on learning ecologies, tacit knowledge, knowledge flows and making creates a practical question: how does relevant knowledge move into work and adapt as conditions change? Repositories and training can preserve and distribute explicit knowledge, but organisations also need ways to observe, practise, exchange, experiment and revise. Knowing, making and playing provide complementary modes for inquiry, not a promise that play alone produces innovation.

### Disciplined workshop design

Fitzpatrick & Hunt's workshop-design principles inform audience profiling, sharp learning outcomes, schedule chunks, activity variety, practical application, scenario evaluation, appropriate breaks and tangible outputs. Knowledge describes what participants need to understand; Skills what they need to practise; Wisdom what they need to judge under uncertainty. No exact 20-minute cadence or outcome guarantee is claimed as universal.

### Human judgment and accountability

Judgment connects strategic choice to organisational learning: people frame problems, evaluate evidence, accept or investigate uncertainty and remain responsible for consequential choices. Behavioural design considers attention, defaults, incentives and whether a person can meaningfully intervene in a workflow. Systems thinking follows consequences and feedback beyond an isolated task.

## Workshop structure

### Strategic Choice Cascade Sprint

For executive, business-unit and strategy leaders. Participants clarify aspiration, where to play, how to win, capabilities and management systems; make exclusions visible; test assumptions using “what would have to be true?” and a Judging Hat challenge. Primary working output: an integrated one-page strategic choice cascade with unresolved assumptions and possible tests. One-day or three-session versions are proposed in the source brief but require confirmation.

### Scalable Learning & Knowledge Flows Lab

For transformation, product, operations and learning leaders, working with practitioners. Participants map knowledge stocks and flows, tacit expertise, relationships and learning opportunities, then sketch a bounded creation space or experiment. Primary working output: an organisational learning and knowledge-flow blueprint. The proposed two-day format requires confirmation.

### Blended Epistemology Studio

For innovation, product and cross-functional leadership teams. Participants move between knowing, making and playing to create strategic hypotheses, scenario prototypes and assumption tests. Primary working outputs: alternative hypotheses, scenario prototypes, a test framework and visual business-model alternatives. The proposed one-day format requires confirmation.

### Executive Offsites & Custom Masterclasses

Combine modules around an agreed leadership decision, capability question or competing hypotheses. Scope participants, facilitation, format and artifacts in advance. The flagship Strategic Choice & Scalable Learning Masterclass is presented as an illustrative sequence only until timing and delivery feasibility are confirmed.

Existing work on judgment under abundance, human–AI workflows, the Agentic Designer and !Khairos is retained as relevant activity, module or follow-up rather than a second parallel offer catalogue.

## Editorial direction

The six archive themes are Strategy and Choice; Learning and Knowledge; AI and Human Judgment; Organisational Integrity; Behavioural Design; and Strategic Experimentation. They are navigation aids, not claims that every essay belongs exclusively to one field.

Editorial work should answer a practical organisational question, show the reasoning and limitations behind an idea, connect it to a decision or practice, and distinguish published argument from evaluated evidence. Proposed articles and videos remain future work until researched, produced and reviewed. The unsupported “80%” headline, implied institutional social proof, `[span_...]` artifacts and unverified claims from the source document are excluded.

## Engagement route

The existing email route remains the primary contact mechanism. Visitors can describe a strategic choice, learning challenge, desired participants and what they hope to decide next. Fees, duration, delivery mode and follow-up are settled in scoping; none is represented as confirmed by this content strategy.
