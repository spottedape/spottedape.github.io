01 / Identify your need

Where is the organisation getting stuck?

Choose the pattern closest to the work. This navigator is a starting point for a conversation, not a scored or validated diagnosis.

  • 01↗The Planning TrapPlans list initiatives, but leaders have not agreed which customers and markets to focus on or how to create a competitive advantage.
  • 02↗Knowledge That Does Not Reach the WorkUseful expertise is stored across repositories, but does not reach changing work.
  • 03↗Tacit Knowledge SilosA few practitioners carry know-how that colleagues cannot learn from a document alone.
  • 04↗Analytical ParalysisAnalysis expands while the choice, assumptions and next useful test stay unclear.
  • 05↗The Innovation DisconnectExperiments produce activity, but do not inform a strategic choice or build capability.
  • 06↗Executive AlignmentLeaders share an ambition but hold different assumptions about trade-offs and capabilities.

03 / One-day masterclass

A day built for decisions, not slide marathons.

An illustrative executive sequence: strategic framing, hypothesis testing, learning flows and a concrete synthesis. Timing and delivery are confirmed during scoping.

  1. 08:45–09:00 · K Arrival and strategic question
  2. 09:00–09:30 · K The Strategy Choice Cascade
  3. 09:30–10:15 · S Choose where to compete—and what to exclude
  4. 10:15–10:30 · Break Coffee
  5. 10:30–11:15 · W Identify the conditions each option requires
  6. 11:15–12:00 · S/W Stakeholder review
  7. 12:00–13:00 · Break Lunch
  8. 13:00–13:45 · K Knowledge stocks and flows
  9. 13:45–14:45 · S Designing a creation space
  10. 14:45–15:00 · Break Afternoon reset
  11. 15:00–16:00 · W Executive choice synthesis

K = Knowledge · S = Skills · W = Wisdom. This indicative schedule is adapted to the audience, work and delivery context. Breaks and activity rhythm are designed for the session rather than treated as universal rules.

04 / Method

Strategy chooses.
Learning builds capability.
Judgement holds the line.

We draw on strategic choice approaches described by A.G. Lafley and Roger Martin, John Seely Brown’s work on learning, and executive learning design. We apply these ideas to decisions, capability and accountability.
