Workshops & Labs

Structured working sessions for leadership teams facing consequential choices, unsettled assumptions or capabilities they need to develop.

Work on the decision, not the deck

A workshop should leave useful work behind.

The engagements below are designed around a real organisational question. Participants examine choices and assumptions, practise a relevant capability and make a working artifact they can take into the next conversation.

Every programme is scoped around its participants, decision, evidence, constraints and intended use. Formats and durations described as proposed below are starting points for discussion, not confirmed delivery commitments. No session by itself guarantees strategic alignment or implementation.

01 / Executive leadership, strategy and business-unit teams

Strategic Choice Cascade Sprint

The problem

An organisation can have extensive planning material without a clear account of where it will compete, how it expects to win, or what it must be willing to stop doing. More analysis may refine the plan while leaving the choices unresolved.

Who should attend

Executive leaders, strategy directors and business-unit leaders who can shape or sponsor a consequential strategic choice. The right participants depend on the arena and capabilities under discussion.

Questions the team works on

  • What is the organisation’s winning aspiration?
  • Where will it play, and where will it not play?
  • How will it create a meaningful reason to choose it?
  • What capabilities must be present for that choice to work?
  • Which management systems need to support those capabilities?

What participants do

The group frames the decision and makes the choice cascade explicit. Leaders map boundaries across relevant customers, needs, geographies or channels, then examine whether the proposed way to win follows from those boundaries. They identify required capabilities and systems rather than treating them as a later implementation list.

For each important choice, participants ask what would have to be true? They turn assumptions into questions that could change the decision. In a Judging Hat exercise, another group takes the position of a selective customer, a capable competitor or a sceptical decision-maker and stress-tests the cascade. Participants distinguish a persuasive argument from an assumption that still needs evidence.

What the team develops

An integrated view of aspiration, where to play, how to win, capabilities and enabling systems; a clearer account of trade-offs; and a short list of assumptions and tests that matter to the choice.

What participants leave with

The primary working output is a one-page strategic choice cascade. It records the connected choices and makes unresolved assumptions visible. It is a basis for further evidence and executive decision-making, not a substitute for that work.

Proposed format

The strategy brief proposes a one-day intensive or a three-session sprint. The suitable format, duration, preparation and follow-up need to be confirmed during scoping.

02 / Transformation, product, operations and learning leaders

Scalable Learning & Knowledge Flows Lab

The problem

Organisations invest in repositories, procedures and training, yet important knowledge may remain hard to find, interpret or apply in changing situations. Experienced practitioners may carry crucial know-how that never travels beyond the immediate team.

Who should attend

Leaders responsible for organisational learning, transformation, product or operations, together with practitioners who understand where work actually happens and how expertise develops.

What participants do

Participants map a knowledge ecology around a concrete organisational challenge. They distinguish codified knowledge from knowledge developed through practice, identify where people learn from one another and trace what happens when knowledge moves between teams, tools and decisions.

The group looks for the conditions that help expertise travel: communities of practice, useful connections to people at the edge of the work, opportunities to observe and try, and places to make or test a new approach. It then designs a bounded creation space or experiment: who takes part, what work they can access, what they may try, what feedback matters and how learning returns to a decision.

Where AI has entered the workflow, participants can map what people and systems produce, which judgments remain consequential, where review or intervention belongs, and which practices should remain available for learning.

What the team develops

A practical account of knowledge stocks and flows, tacit expertise, relationships and learning conditions. The focus is not to replace repositories or conventional training, but to connect them to use, feedback and adaptation.

What participants leave with

A knowledge-flow and organisational-learning blueprint showing a priority challenge, relevant people and knowledge, important hand-offs, proposed creation spaces or learning practices, and questions to test. Depending on scope, this may include a Human Agency Map or a workflow and decision-rights view.

Proposed format

The strategy brief proposes a two-day applied lab. Duration, participation and delivery mode are unconfirmed and should be agreed around the problem and available evidence.

03 / Innovation, product and cross-functional leadership teams

Blended Epistemology Studio

The problem

Teams may keep optimising a familiar model because evidence and established categories make it easier to improve what is already legible than to imagine a different arena. Alternatively, exploratory activity can produce concepts without a path to a strategic decision.

Who should attend

Innovation leaders, product strategists and cross-functional teams with a live strategic question or uncertain opportunity to examine.

What participants do

The studio works across three modes: knowing, bringing evidence and experience into view; making, giving a possibility a form that can be examined; and playing, changing perspective and reframing the question.

Participants name what they know, what remains uncertain and what they currently assume. They then sketch alternatives to the existing model, develop an abductive hypothesis about what might work and build a scenario prototype that makes the idea discussable. A Judging Hat review tests it from another position. The team asks what would have to be true, what could falsify the hypothesis and what bounded experiment could inform a next choice.

Making a prototype is not presented as evidence that an idea will work. It gives people something concrete to challenge, compare and revise.

What the team develops

The ability to move between analysis, imagination and practical testing without confusing a plausible story with validated evidence.

What participants leave with

Alternative strategic hypotheses, scenario prototypes, an assumption-testing framework and visual sketches of possible business models. The final set depends on the question and the work completed in the session.

Proposed format

The strategy brief proposes a one-day studio. The format and time required depend on the scope of the opportunity and whether follow-up testing is included.

04 / Senior leadership and mixed stakeholder groups

Executive Offsites & Custom Masterclasses

An offsite is useful when a group needs protected time to work through a real decision, not simply to discuss strategy in a different room. A custom masterclass can introduce a shared language and then move directly into application, scenario work and synthesis.

We agree the decision, participant profile, learning outcomes, material, format, facilitation role and intended outputs in advance. Modules can be combined from the choice cascade, knowledge-flow lab and blended studio. Existing work on judgment under abundance, human–AI workflows, the Agentic Designer and the !Khairos capability progression can inform activities or follow-up where relevant; they are not a second product catalogue.

Outputs are agreed for the engagement. They may include a choice cascade, explicit assumptions and tests, a knowledge-flow blueprint, scenario prototypes or decision ownership. A workshop artifact records work completed in the room; it does not imply executive endorsement, implementation or a guaranteed result.

Illustrative programme / timing to confirm

Strategic Choice & Scalable Learning Masterclass

The supplied curriculum combines a strategy choice cascade with a practical inquiry into the capabilities and knowledge flows needed to act. It is designed for senior leaders, general managers and strategy directors. The sequence below is an illustrative programme skeleton, not a confirmed schedule or a claim that one day is sufficient for every strategic question.

  1. 09:00–09:30 · Strategic framing and the choice cascadeClarify the decision; introduce aspiration, where to play, how to win, capabilities and management systems.
  2. 09:30–10:15 · Where to play and where not to playMap boundaries across relevant customers, needs, channels or geographies; make exclusions visible.
  3. 10:15–10:30 · BreakProtected pause and room reset.
  4. 10:30–11:15 · What would have to be true?Identify assumptions behind a strategic choice and evidence that could test them.
  5. 11:15–12:00 · Judging Hat stress-testReview a team’s cascade from another stakeholder’s position; surface gaps, risks and unanswered questions.
  6. 12:00–13:00 · LunchProtected break.
  7. 13:00–13:45 · Knowledge stocks and flowsExplore what is codified, what is learned through practice and where knowledge must move.
  8. 13:45–14:45 · Designing a creation spaceMap tacit knowledge and relationships; sketch a bounded setting for making, exchange and experimentation.
  9. 14:45–15:00 · BreakProtected pause.
  10. 15:00–16:00 · Executive synthesisRevise the cascade, state unresolved assumptions and agree proportionate next questions and owners.

The learning design distinguishes Knowledge (what participants need to understand), Skills (what they need to practise) and Wisdom (what they need to judge under uncertainty). It varies explanation, individual and group application, scenario challenge and synthesis to serve those outcomes. The precise rhythm, breaks, facilitation and timetable must be adapted to participants and delivery context.

The intended core output is a working one-page choice cascade, accompanied by assumptions to test and a first view of the learning or capability conditions the choices require. It is not represented as a signed-off strategy or a validated diagnostic.

How we design the work

Specific outcomes. Structured practice. Useful artifacts.

Audience profile and learning outcomes shape the activity. Concepts are introduced only where they help participants do the work. Practice is followed by discussion or critique; scenarios let participants examine choices and consequences from a different position. Breaks and format changes are planned to suit the session rather than imposed as a universal rhythm.

The work draws on strategy-as-choice, organisational learning and executive-learning design. It retains The Spotted Ape’s concern with judgment, human agency and the consequences of delegating work. It does not imply a formal partnership with the scholars or institutions whose ideas inform it.

Before we begin

Practical questions

Is this AI tools training?

Participants practise framing, evaluating and justifying decisions, and design where people and machines may act. Tool use supports those activities; the sessions are not a tour of AI features.

Do participants need technical knowledge?

No coding knowledge is required. We agree the starting point in advance, including participants’ experience with AI.

Is it tied to ChatGPT or a particular platform?

The framework is platform-independent. Tool choices for exercises are agreed with your organisation.

Can we use our own workflows?

Yes. We can work with a relevant workflow or document, subject to agreed confidentiality and tool-use boundaries. An anonymised or synthetic example is an alternative.

Is it suitable for leadership teams?

The briefing helps leaders separate production from judgment and identify capabilities at risk of erosion. A workflow lab can examine how their team reviews work, delegates decisions and responds to failure.

Can workshops be delivered virtually?

Discuss the proposed format, group size and collaboration needs with us. Delivery arrangements are agreed before an engagement is confirmed.

How is confidentiality handled?

We agree what material can be used, which platforms are permitted and what must be anonymised or excluded. Do not send confidential documents with an initial enquiry.

Can it fit our existing AI policies?

Yes. Existing policies and governance requirements become constraints for the exercises and workflow design. Specific arrangements are agreed during scoping.
